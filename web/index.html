Welcome to CF Evolution

CF Evolution is an open-source editor for Geoff Crammond's Formula One Grand Prix and World Circuit — the classic 1991 MicroProse racing simulation. It allows you to edit track layouts, best line data, car and team settings, and more, all from a cross-platform Java application.

CF Evolution is the successor to the Chequered Flag project. It builds on the Chequered Flag 0.1.1 codebase with significant bug fixes, new features, and ongoing development. The project is free and open-source software, licensed under the GNU General Public License v2.

What’s New in 0.2.0

Version 0.2.0 brings a substantial update over the ChequeredFlag 0.1.x baseline, with improvements to visualization, usability, and correctness:

  • Game-accurate best line (CCLine) visualization — the CCLine overlay now uses the same lateral curvature formula as the game engine, giving a faithful representation of where the AI will drive.
  • Out-of-bounds CCLine indicator — segments where the best line falls outside the drivable track boundary are highlighted in orange, using the game’s own track width formula and car-width margin.
  • Pit lane display — the pit lane is now rendered on the track map, connecting entry to exit, with the selected pit segment highlighted.
  • Safe radius guidance — the CCLine segment editor shows a live safe radius indicator, warning when a curved sector’s radius is too small for its length.
  • Improved mouse navigation — plain left-button drag pans the map; scroll wheel zooms centred on the cursor position.
  • Bug fixes — wide-radius CCLine operator precedence error; ExtraSideX/Y self-assignment; CCLine wrap-around off-by-one; pit lane leaf/teardrop shape; Pythagorean overflow on very small CCLine radii.

About the Game

Formula One Grand Prix (known as World Circuit in North America) was released by MicroProse in 1991 and designed by Geoff Crammond. It set a new standard for racing simulation at the time, featuring all 16 circuits of the 1991 F1 season, accurate car physics, and detailed AI behaviour. Despite its age, the game retains a dedicated community of enthusiasts who continue to develop new tracks, car sets, and tools.
